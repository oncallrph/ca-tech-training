Module 1 of 6 • Estimated Reading Time: 20 Minutes Cleanroom Architecture

Cleanroom Design, ISO Classifications & USP <797>

Compounded Sterile Preparations (CSPs) require strict environmental controls to prevent microbial contamination. **USP <797>** governs the policies, physical environments, and quality controls necessary for sterile compounding.

1.1 ISO Air Cleanliness Classifications

Air cleanliness is classified by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) based on the maximum allowable particle count (≥ 0.5 µm) per cubic meter (m³) of air:

ISO Class 5

Primary Engineering Control (PEC)

Maximum 3,520 particles/m³. Directly surrounds critical sites during compounding.

  • Laminar Airflow Workbenches (LAFW)
  • Biological Safety Cabinets (BSC)
  • Compounding Aseptic Isolators (CAI)
ISO Class 7

Buffer Area (Cleanroom)

Maximum 352,000 particles/m³. Room housing the PECs where sterile preparation occurs.

  • HEPA-filtered air ceiling diffusers
  • No sinks or floor drains near PEC
  • Positive air pressure (non-hazardous)
ISO Class 8

Ante-Area (Gowning & Prep)

Maximum 3,520,000 particles/m³. Transition zone between unclassified pharmacy & buffer room.

  • Garbing & hand hygiene station
  • Order entry & component staging
  • Demarcation line separating clean side

1.2 Air Pressure Differentials: Positive vs. Negative Pressure

Positive Pressure Cleanroom (Non-Hazardous)

Air flows OUT of the buffer room into the ante-room (higher pressure to lower pressure) to prevent dirty air from entering the sterile preparation space. Minimum pressure differential: 0.02 to 0.05 inches water column.

Negative Pressure Cleanroom (Hazardous USP <800>)

Air flows INWARD toward the compounding room to trap hazardous chemotherapy vapors inside. Air is vented externally through HEPA filters to protect pharmacy personnel.

Module 2 of 6 • Estimated Reading Time: 25 Minutes Garbing & Hygiene

Personnel Garbing, Hand Hygiene & Gloved Fingertip Testing

Human personnel are the leading source of particle shed and contamination in sterile compounding. Rigorous garbing and antiseptic hand cleansing protocols are mandatory under **USP <797>**.

2.1 The Dirtiest-to-Cleanest Garbing Order

Garbing must proceed in order from the dirtiest parts of the body to the cleanest to prevent cross-contamination:

Step 1: Dedicated Shoe Covers (or dedicated cleanroom shoes) Ante-Room
Step 2: Head & Facial Hair Covers (beard covers) Ante-Room
Step 3: Face Mask & Eye Shield (if required) Ante-Room
Step 4: Antiseptic Hand Cleansing (30 sec scrub up to elbows) Sink Station
Step 5: Non-Shedding Cleanroom Gown (snug cuffs, tied back) Clean Side of Ante-Room
Step 6: Alcohol Hand Rub (70% IPA) & Dry Entering Buffer Room
Step 7: Sterile Powder-Free Gloves (over gown cuffs) Inside Buffer Room / PEC

2.2 Antiseptic Hand Wash & Gloved Fingertip Testing (GFT)

Hand Washing Technique:

Remove jewelry, rings, and watches. Clean nails under warm running water using a single-use nail cleaner. Wash hands and forearms up to the elbows for at least **30 seconds** with soap and water. Dry with non-shedding disposable towels.

Gloved Fingertip & Thumb Sampling:

Compounding personnel must pass Gloved Fingertip Testing (GFT) on agar plates. Initial competency requires **0 Colony Forming Units (CFU)** on both hands (3 consecutive passes). Re-evaluation occurs annually (Category 1/2) or semi-annually (Category 3/Hazardous).

Module 3 of 6 • Estimated Reading Time: 20 Minutes Aseptic Manipulations

Aseptic Technique, Critical Sites & Equipment Manipulation

Aseptic technique is the procedure of compounding sterile products without introducing contamination. The core rule is maintaining **First Air** over all **Critical Sites**.

3.1 Concept of First Air & Critical Site Identification

**First Air** is the direct, unobstructed air exiting the HEPA filter inside the ISO Class 5 Primary Engineering Control (PEC).

Universal Critical Sites (Never Touch or Block First Air!)
  • Needle: Hub, shaft, bevel tip, and bevel heel.
  • Syringe: Syringe tip, inner plunger shaft/ribs (holding the flat plunger end is permitted).
  • Vial: Rubber stopper after swabbing with 70% IPA.
  • IV Bag: Injection additive port and spike port membrane.
  • Glass Ampule: Open neck edge after snapping.

3.2 Proper Swabbing and Solution Manipulations

Vial Pressure Equalization:

Vials are closed systems. To withdraw liquid without creating a vacuum, inject a volume of air equal to the desired liquid volume into the vial before withdrawing solution (except when working with hazardous cytotoxic drugs).

Glass Ampule Manipulation:

Ampules are open systems. Swab the neck with 70% IPA, snap away from the HEPA filter. Always use a **5-micron filter needle or filter straw** when withdrawing liquid to prevent glass fragments from entering the final IV bag.

Quality Assurance, Environmental Testing & USP <797> BUDs

Quality Assurance (QA) in sterile compounding encompasses environmental monitoring, media-fill sterility testing, particulate verification, and Beyond-Use Date (BUD) calculations.

4.1 Environmental Monitoring & Competency Requirements

Test Type Minimum Frequency Pass Criteria / Action Level
Air Sampling (Viable Particles) Every 6 Months ISO 5: > 1 CFU/m³; ISO 7: > 10 CFU; ISO 8: > 100 CFU triggers action
Surface Sampling (Touch Plates) Monthly (or per USP guidelines) ISO 5: > 3 CFU/plate; ISO 7: > 5 CFU; ISO 8: > 50 CFU triggers cleaning
Media Fill Testing Annually (Category 1 & 2), Semi-Annually (Cat 3/Haz) 14-day incubation using Soybean-Casein Digest Medium; zero turbidity (growth)

4.2 USP <797> CSP Categories & Beyond-Use Dates (BUD)

Under revised USP <797> standards, Compounded Sterile Preparations (CSPs) are classified into **Category 1** or **Category 2** (and Category 3 for extended stability):

Category 1 CSPs (SCA Environment)
Controlled Room Temp: ≤ 12 Hours
Refrigerated: ≤ 24 Hours

Prepared in an unclassified Segregated Compounding Area (SCA) inside an ISO 5 PEC.

Category 2 CSPs (Cleanroom Suite)
Room Temp: Up to 4 Days
Refrigerated: Up to 10 Days
Frozen: Up to 45 Days

Aseptically prepared with sterile components in an ISO 7 Buffer Room + ISO 8 Ante-Room (without sterility testing).

Module 5 of 6 • Estimated Reading Time: 20 Minutes Calculations Laboratory

Sterile Compounding Mathematics & Infusion Rate Lab

Sterile compounding math requires precise mastery of infusion flow rates (mL/hr), drip rates (gtt/min), electrolyte conversions (mEq), and powder displacement volumes.

Key IV Formulas Quick Reference

IV Drip Rate Formula:
$$\text{Drip Rate (gtt/min)} = \frac{\text{Volume (mL)} \times \text{Drop Factor (gtt/mL)}}{\text{Time (minutes)}}$$
Infusion Duration Formula:
$$\text{Time (hours)} = \frac{\text{Total Bag Volume (mL)}}{\text{Flow Rate (mL/hr)}}$$
