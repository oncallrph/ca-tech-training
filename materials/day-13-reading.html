Module 1 • 20 Minutes Target

Prescription Sources & Legal Intake

Understanding origin channels, mandatory legal components, and California-specific intake scope rules.

1. Prescription Origin Channels

Prescriptions enter the pharmacy workflow via four distinct channels, each governed by specific legal protocols:

1. Written / Hardcopy

Presented in person by the patient or agent. Must be written in indelible ink or printed. Non-controlled medications may be written on standard prescription pads; controlled substances require official California Security Prescription forms.

2. Electronic Prescriptions (eRx)

Transmitted directly from prescriber EHR/EMR software via encrypted pharmacy networks (e.g., Surescripts). Mandatory in California under Assembly Bill 2789 (effective Jan 1, 2021) for almost all prescriptions with narrow statutory exceptions.

3. Faxed Prescriptions

Must be transmitted directly from the prescriber's official office fax. The faxed document serves as the original hardcopy. Must include date, sender fax number, and office header. Faxed C-II scripts are generally prohibited except for hospice or long-term care facility patients.

4. Verbal / Telephone Orders

Phoned in by the prescriber or authorized agent. CA Scope Rule: Under California Business and Professions Code, ONLY a Licensed Pharmacist or Pharmacist Intern may take NEW verbal prescriptions. Pharmacy Technicians cannot take phone orders for new scripts!

2. Mandatory Statutory Requirements (CA B&PC § 4040)

Every valid prescription in California must contain specific statutory elements before processing:

California Statutory Elements Checklist:
  • Patient Full Name & Address: (Address must be recorded in system profile or on prescription).
  • Drug Name, Strength, & Quantity: Exact item, dosage form, and numerical quantity.
  • Directions for Use (SIG): Specific instructions describing method, route, dosage, and frequency.
  • Date of Issuance: Date signed by prescriber. Non-controlled scripts expire in 1 year; C-II to C-V expire in 6 months.
  • Prescriber Information: Name, professional address, phone number, and license classification.
  • Prescriber Signature: Physical ink signature for paper, or encrypted digital signature for eRx.
  • DEA Registration Number: Mandatory if the prescription is for a Controlled Substance (Schedules II–V).

Module 2 • 25 Minutes Target

Data Entry, SIG Decoding & DAW Selection

Translating Latin SIG abbreviations, verifying profiles, and assigning Dispense As Written (DAW) codes.

1. Patient Profile Linking

Accurate computer entry begins with linking the order to the correct patient file. Crucial data points include:

  • Full Legal Name & Date of Birth (DOB): Essential for differentiating patients with identical names.
  • Allergies & Sensitivities: Document all drug allergies. Record NKDA (No Known Drug Allergies) if none exist.
  • Physical Address & Telephone Number: Mandatory for reporting and patient notifications.
  • Insurance Credentials: BIN, PCN, Group Number, Member ID, and Person Code.

2. SIG Code Abbreviation Dictionary

Technicians must translate Latin medical shorthand into standard English instructions for prescription labels:

SIG Abbreviation Latin Term Meaning Patient Label Translation
1 tab po tid pc per os / ter in die / post cibum 1 tablet by mouth 3 times daily after meals Take 1 tablet by mouth 3 times daily after meals.
1 gtt ou q4h prn oculus uterque / quaque 4 hora 1 drop in both eyes every 4 hours as needed Instill 1 drop into both eyes every 4 hours as needed.
2 puff qid prn sob quater in die / pro re nata 2 puffs 4 times daily as needed for shortness of breath Inhale 2 puffs 4 times daily as needed for shortness of breath.
1 tab po hs hora somni 1 tablet by mouth at bedtime Take 1 tablet by mouth at bedtime.
1 supp pv qhs per vaginam / quaque hora somni 1 suppository vaginally every night at bedtime Insert 1 suppository vaginally every night at bedtime.

3. Dispense As Written (DAW) Codes

NCPDP DAW codes signal to insurance processors why a generic or brand product was selected:

DAW 0: No Product Selection Indicated
Default setting. Prescriber wrote generic or authorized substitution. Generic is dispensed.
DAW 1: Substitution Not Allowed by Prescriber
Prescriber mandated brand name product (e.g., wrote "Do Not Substitute" or marked DAW box).
DAW 2: Patient Requested Brand Product
Prescriber allowed generic, but the patient explicitly requested brand. Patient may incur higher copay.
DAW 4: Generic Out of Stock
Generic is permitted, but pharmacy is temporarily out of generic inventory and dispenses brand product.

Module 3 • 20 Minutes Target

Insurance Billing & Rejection Resolution

Understanding Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) cards and resolving online claim rejections.

1. Insurance Credentials

Entering insurance data requires five specific parameters to route claims electronically:

1. BIN (Bank Identification Number)
6-digit routing code identifying the PBM processor (e.g., Express Scripts, CVS Caremark).
2. PCN (Processor Control Number)
Secondary alphanumeric code routing claims to specific benefit plan sub-networks.
3. Group Number
Identifies the specific employer or plan sponsor purchasing the health coverage.
4. Member ID / Subscriber ID
Unique alphanumeric code assigned to the primary insured cardholder.
5. Person Code (Relationship Code)
Defines individual beneficiary: 01 = Cardholder, 02 = Spouse, 03 = First Child, etc.

2. Resolving Common Claim Rejections

Rejection Code 79: "Refill Too Soon"
Cause: Attempting to fill before 75% to 85% of previous day supply has elapsed.
Action: Check remaining doses with patient. If dosage was changed, obtain updated SIG prescription. If traveling, submit a "Vacation Override" code (SCC 03).
Rejection Code 75: "Prior Authorization Required"
Cause: Drug is non-formulary, high cost, or requires clinical justification.
Action: Notify prescriber's office via electronic PA system (CoverMyMeds) and inform patient of delay.
Rejection: "Plan Limitation / Quantity Exceeded"
Cause: Prescribed quantity exceeds maximum allowable units per 30-day period.
Action: Adjust quantity to match plan cap or request PA override.

Module 4 • 20 Minutes Target

Controlled Substance Processing & DEA Verification

Schedule II-V legal rules, California security paper specifications, and DEA check-digit calculations.

1. Controlled Substance Schedule Rules

Schedule II (C-II) High Abuse Potential
  • Drugs: Oxycodone, Hydrocodone/APAP (Norco), Adderall, Methylphenidate, Fentanyl.
  • Refills: ZERO Refills Allowed.
  • Validity: Valid for 6 months from date written in California.
  • Telephone Scripts: Emergency situations only (RPh directly). Written security paper copy required within 7 days.
Schedule III–V (C-III, C-IV, C-V) Moderate-Low Abuse
  • Drugs: Tylenol #3 (C-III), Alprazolam/Xanax (C-IV), Tramadol (C-IV), Lyrica (C-V).
  • Refills: Maximum of 5 Refills within 6 months.
  • Quantity Cap: Total refills combined cannot exceed a 120-day supply total.
  • Validity: Valid for 6 months from issue date.

2. DEA Check Digit Formula

Every DEA registration number consists of 2 letters followed by 7 digits (e.g., AB1234567):

First Letter: Identifies registrant (A, B, F, G = Doctors/Pharmacies; M = Mid-level Practitioners NP/PA).
Second Letter: First letter of prescriber's last name.
Step 1: (1st + 3rd + 5th digits)
Step 2: (2nd + 4th + 6th digits) × 2
Step 3: Sum of Step 1 + Sum of Step 2
Rule: Last digit of Step 3 total MUST match 7th digit of DEA number.

Module 5 • 20 Minutes Target

Day Supply & Prescription Math Lab

Precision calculations for oral solids, eye/ear drop conversions, and insulin pen/vial units.

1. Core Day Supply Equation

Day Supply Formula
Day Supply = Total Quantity Dispensed ÷ (Doses per Day × Units per Dose)

2. Ophthalmic & Otic Drop Conversions

Standard Drop Metric: 1 mL = 20 drops (gtts)

Sample Problem: Timolol 0.5% Ophthalmic Solution. SIG: Instill 1 drop OU bid. Bottle size: 10 mL. Calculate day supply.

Total drops in bottle = 10 mL × 20 drops/mL = 200 drops
Daily usage = 1 drop × 2 eyes × 2 times/day = 4 drops/day
Day Supply = 200 drops ÷ 4 drops/day = 50 Days

3. Insulin Vial & Pen Math Rules

  • Concentration: U-100 insulin = 100 units/mL.
  • Vials (10 mL): 10 mL × 100 units/mL = 1,000 units total per vial.
  • Pens (3 mL each): 3 mL × 100 units/mL = 300 units per pen (Box of 5 pens = 1,500 units total).
  • Expiration Rule: Opened room-temperature insulin vials/pens expire after 28 days regardless of remaining volume.
