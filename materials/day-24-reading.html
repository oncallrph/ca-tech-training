Module 1 • 20 Minutes Target

Recognizing Medication Emergencies & Acute Triage

Differentiating minor adverse reactions from life-threatening medical emergencies in ambulatory and institutional settings.

1. Life-Threatening Emergencies vs. Minor Adverse Effects

Pharmacy technicians are frequently the first healthcare staff member a patient or caregiver approaches when experiencing unexpected symptoms. Differentiating benign side effects from systemic emergencies is vital:

Emergency Condition Key Clinical Symptoms Common Medication Causes Immediate Action Required
Anaphylaxis (IgE-Mediated) Urticaria (hives), angioedema (swelling of lips/tongue/throat), wheezing, hypotension, dyspnea. Penicillins, Cephalosporins, NSAIDs, Biologics, Sulfa drugs. Immediate Epinephrine IM, alert Pharmacist, call 911.
Severe Hypoglycemia Diaphoresis (sweating), tremors, confusion, dizziness, seizures, loss of consciousness ($< 54\text{ mg/dL}$). Insulins, Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glyburide). Oral fast-acting glucose if conscious; Glucagon / $D_{50}W$ if unconscious.
Opioid Overdose Unresponsiveness, respiratory depression ($< 8-10\text{ breaths/min}$), pinpoint pupils (miosis), cyanosis. Morphine, Oxycodone, Fentanyl, Hydrocodone, Methadone. Administer Naloxone (Narcan) IN, call 911, rescue breathing.
Acute Coronary Syndrome / Angina Substernal chest pain/pressure radiating to jaw or left arm, dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea. Nitroglycerin withdrawal, stimulant misuse, acute ischemic event. Alert Pharmacist, call 911, assist patient to sit, assist with sublingual Nitroglycerin if prescribed.

2. Anaphylaxis Clinical Triage Pathway

Anaphylaxis is an acute, life-threatening multi-organ allergic reaction. It typically involves two or more body systems within minutes of drug exposure:

Respiratory Compromise
Shortness of breath, stridor, wheezing, throat tightness, persistent cough, inability to speak full sentences.
Cardiovascular Dysfunction
Hypotension (systolic BP drops below $90\text{ mmHg}$), syncope, dizziness, weak thready pulse, cardiovascular collapse.
Mucocutaneous Symptoms
Widespread hives (urticaria), severe itching (pruritus), swelling of lip, tongue, uvula, and periorbital tissue.

Module 2 • 25 Minutes Target

Opioid Toxicity Recognition & Naloxone (Narcan) Protocol

Identifying the opioid overdose triad, mechanism of action, California standing orders, and administration guidelines.

1. The Opioid Overdose Triad

Opioids (e.g., Fentanyl, Oxycodone, Morphine, Heroin) suppress the brainstem respiratory control center. Overdose is characterized by three pathognomonic signs:

1. Deep Unresponsiveness
Patient does not respond to verbal stern, shaking, or sternal rub stimulation.
2. Respiratory Depression
Shallow, infrequent breathing ($< 8\text{ breaths/min}$), gurgling/snoring sounds ("death rattle"), or apnea.
3. Pinpoint Pupils (Miosis)
Extremely constricted pupils that do not react to changes in light.

2. Naloxone (Narcan) Pharmacology & Administration

Naloxone is a pure competitive opioid receptor antagonist. It displaces opioids from $\mu$-opioid receptors in the brain, restoring spontaneous respiration within $2-3$ minutes.

Step-by-Step Intranasal Naloxone ($4\text{ mg}$) Protocol:
  1. Call 911 Immediately: Activate EMS before or simultaneously with administration.
  2. Peel Package: Remove Narcan nasal spray from blister packaging. Do not prime or test the device!
  3. Position Nozzle: Place nozzle tip into one nostril until fingers touch the bottom of the patient's nose.
  4. Press Plunger: Press the center plunger firmly to release the full $4\text{ mg}$ dose.
  5. Evaluate & Repeat: If no response after $2-3\text{ minutes}$, administer a second dose into the opposite nostril using a new device.
  6. Rescue Breathing & Recovery Position: Perform rescue breathing if trained; turn patient onto their side (recovery position) to prevent aspiration if vomiting occurs.

3. California Pharmacy Protocol & Legal Safeguards

Under California Business and Professions Code § 4052.01 and State Board protocols, pharmacists can furnish Naloxone without an individual prescription. Pharmacy technicians play a key role in keeping Naloxone stocked, accessible, and ready for immediate walk-in emergency dispensing.

Module 3 • 20 Minutes Target

Calling Emergency Assistance & Poison Control Hotline Protocols

Structured 911 communication using SBAR, Poison Control Center (1-800-222-1222) workflows, and incident documentation.

1. Structured 911 Dispatch Communication (SBAR)

When calling 911 for a medical emergency in the pharmacy, communicating critical information clearly saves valuable seconds. Use the SBAR framework:

S - Situation State your name, exact pharmacy location, address, and the nature of the emergency (e.g., "Anaphylactic reaction in 45-year-old male").
B - Background State relevant medical history, trigger event (e.g., vaccine administered, drug ingested), and patient age/weight.
A - Assessment Current vital signs, airway status, level of consciousness, respiratory rate, and physical physical appearance.
R - Recommendation / Actions Taken Interventions initiated (e.g., "Epinephrine $0.3\text{ mg}$ IM given at 14:02; CPR in progress").

2. Poison Control Hotline Guidelines

The Poison Help line connects healthcare professionals and the public directly to medical toxicologists and specialist pharmacists:

Poison Control Hotline: 1-800-222-1222

When calling Poison Control regarding an accidental ingestion, overdose, or exposure, gather the following details before dialing:

  • Exact substance name, brand, formulation, and strength ($mg$, $\%$, concentration).
  • Estimated quantity involved (e.g., number of tablets missing, volume in $mL$).
  • Time elapsed since exposure or ingestion.
  • Patient age, weight, symptoms, and pre-existing medical conditions.

Module 4 • 20 Minutes Target

Providing Initial Aid, Epinephrine Autoinjectors & BLS Principles

Epinephrine auto-injector mechanics, shock management, positioning, and Basic Life Support (BLS) integration.

1. Epinephrine Auto-Injector Administration Mechanics

Epinephrine is an $\alpha_1, \beta_1, \beta_2$ adrenergic agonist. It reverses bronchospasm ($\beta_2$), reduces mucosal swelling/angioedema ($\alpha_1$), and supports blood pressure ($\beta_1$). Speed of administration is essential:

Adult vs. Pediatric Dosing
  • Adult Dose ($\ge 30\text{ kg}$ / $66\text{ lbs}$): $0.3\text{ mg}$ Epinephrine IM ($1:1,000$ concentration).
  • Pediatric Dose ($15 - 30\text{ kg}$ / $33 - 66\text{ lbs}$): $0.15\text{ mg}$ Epinephrine IM ($1:1,000$ concentration).
  • Infant Dose ($7.5 - 15\text{ kg}$): $0.1\text{ mg}$ (e.g., Auvi-Q 0.1 mg).
Key Injection Steps (EpiPen)
  1. Grasp unit with orange tip down; remove blue safety release cap.
  2. Jab firmly into outer mid-thigh (anterolateral thigh) at a $90^\circ$ angle (can inject through clothing).
  3. Hold firmly in place for 3 full seconds.
  4. Remove and massage injection site for 10 seconds.

2. Patient Positioning & Shock Management

Proper patient positioning during an anaphylactic or hypotensive emergency prevents sudden postural fatal collapse:

Positioning Rules in Anaphylactic Shock:
  • Recumbent / Supine with Legs Elevated: Place patient flat on back with legs raised $8-12$ inches to support venous return to the heart.
  • Respiratory Distress: If patient is struggling to breathe, allow them to sit upright, but never allow them to stand up or walk abruptly.
  • Vomiting / Unconscious: Turn patient onto their side (recovery position) to prevent airway aspiration.

Module 5 • 20 Minutes Target

Clinical Emergency Math & Drug Information Case Lab

Weight-based emergency dosing ($mg/kg$), Epinephrine concentration ratios, and resuscitation fluid rate calculations.

1. Weight-Based Pediatric Emergency Calculations ($mg/kg$)

In pediatric emergency care, drug dosing is strictly based on body weight in kilograms ($kg$):

\(\text{Weight in kg} = \frac{\text{Weight in lbs}}{2.2}\)
Sample Problem: A pediatric patient weighing $44\text{ lbs}$ requires an emergency dose of Epinephrine at $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ IM ($1:1,000$ concentration). What volume in $mL$ should be administered?
1. Weight conversion: \(\frac{44\text{ lbs}}{2.2} = 20\text{ kg}\)
2. Total dose in mg: \(20\text{ kg} \times 0.01\text{ mg/kg} = 0.2\text{ mg}\)
3. Concentration of $1:1,000 = 1\text{ mg/mL}$
4. Volume to inject: \(\frac{0.2\text{ mg}}{1\text{ mg/mL}} = 0.2\text{ mL}\)

2. Understanding Epinephrine Ratio Concentrations ($1:1,000$ vs $1:10,000$)

Epinephrine ratio strength expressions represent grams of drug per milliliter of solution. Confusing these preparations can lead to fatal dosing errors!

Ratio Expression Concentration (mg/mL) Standard Route of Administration Clinical Indication
1:1,000 $1\text{ g} / 1,000\text{ mL} = 1\text{ mg/mL}$ Intramuscular (IM) / Subcutaneous Anaphylaxis, severe asthma exacerbation.
1:10,000 $1\text{ g} / 10,000\text{ mL} = 0.1\text{ mg/mL}$ Intravenous (IV) Push / IO Cardiac arrest, Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS).
