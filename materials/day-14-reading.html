Module 1 • 20 Minutes Target

Medication Verification & Final Check Workflows

Mastering the 5 Rights of Dispensing, National Drug Code (NDC) matching, and barcode verification.

1. The 5 Rights of Medication Dispensing

Before any medication is prepared or handed to a patient, pharmacy personnel must verify the "5 Rights" to prevent adverse drug events:

1. Right Patient
Verify full legal name and date of birth (DOB) using 2 unique identifiers.
2. Right Medication
Match generic/brand name and NDC number exactly against stock container.
3. Right Dose & Strength
Confirm correct numerical dosage (e.g., $10\text{ mg}$ vs $20\text{ mg}$) and dosage form.
4. Right Route
Ensure administration route (Oral, Topical, Ophthalmic, Otic) matches prescription order.
5. Right Time & Quantity
Verify daily administration schedule and accurate total unit count dispensed.

2. National Drug Code (NDC) Structure

The FDA assigns every drug product a unique 10-digit or 11-digit 3-segment number formatted as $5-4-2$ (Labeler - Product Code - Package Code):

00093 Labeler / Manufacturer
-
0154 Product / Strength / Form
-
01 Package Size

Critical Verification Rule: The middle 4 digits identify the specific drug entity and strength. Matching middle digits is mandatory during product selection!

Module 2 • 25 Minutes Target

Prescription Labeling & Container Packaging Specifications

California CCR § 1707.5 label requirements, auxiliary warnings, and Poison Prevention Packaging Act (PPPA) rules.

1. Mandatory Container Label Elements (CA CCR § 1707.5)

California law mandates patient-centered prescription container labels printed in at least 12-point sans-serif font highlighting 4 core elements:

California Patient-Centered Label Elements:
  • Patient Legal Name: First and last name.
  • Drug Name & Strength: Generic name + manufacturer, or trade name.
  • Directions for Use (SIG): Phrase in plain, clear language.
  • Condition or Purpose: Included if requested by patient or indicated on script.
  • Mandatory Legal Additions: Prescription number (Rx #), quantity, issuance date, expiration date, prescriber name, and pharmacy name/address.

2. Poison Prevention Packaging Act (PPPA) of 1970

Enforced by the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC), the PPPA mandates child-resistant (CR) packaging for all oral prescription medications:

CR Standard Performance

Packaging must prevent at least 80% of children under 5 years from opening it within 10 minutes, while allowing at least 90% of adults to open it easily.

Statutory Exemptions

Sublingual Nitroglycerin (SL NTG) is ALWAYS exempt (requires rapid emergency access). Oral contraceptives in dial-packs, cholestyramine powder, and potassium unit-dose are also exempt.

Easy-Open Cap Waiver Rules:
  • Patient Request: May request a signed blanket waiver covering all future prescriptions.
  • Prescriber Request: May request easy-open caps for a single individual prescription only (cannot issue a blanket waiver for all patient scripts).

Module 3 • 20 Minutes Target

Patient Consultation Scope & Communication Protocols

California Duty to Consult (CCR § 1707.2), technician scope limits, and facilitating pharmacist consultation.

1. California Mandatory Consultation Rule (16 CCR § 1707.2)

Under California Board of Pharmacy regulations, a pharmacist MUST provide consultation to the patient or patient's agent in the following circumstances:

1. All New Prescriptions
Mandatory consultation whenever a prescription is filled for the first time.
2. Refills with Changes
Whenever a prescription is refilled in a different dosage form, strength, or written direction.
3. Patient Request
Whenever the patient or agent explicitly requests consultation with the pharmacist.
4. Pharmacist Clinical Judgment
Whenever the pharmacist determines consultation is necessary for patient safety.

2. Technician Legal Scope & Pickup Counter Rules

STRICT SCOPE RESTRICTION FOR TECHNICIANS:

A Pharmacy Technician CANNOT answer clinical questions, advise on drug side effects, explain usage directions, or recommend OTC medications. Technicians may only facilitate the offer to consult!

  • Correct Pickup Phrase: "The pharmacist is required to go over this new prescription with you. Let me call them over."
  • Prohibited Action: Asking "Do you have any questions, or are you good?" in a manner that discourages consultation.

Module 4 • 20 Minutes Target

Controlled Substance Dispensing & Identification Rules

Government ID verification, electronic signature logs, CURES 2.0 reporting, and Schedule II partial fills.

1. Pickup Verification & Government Photo ID

To prevent diversion and fraudulent pick-ups of controlled substances (Schedules II–V), California pharmacies enforce strict identification protocols:

Valid Photo ID Requirements
  • Unexpired US State Driver's License or State ID.
  • US Passport or Foreign Government Passport.
  • US Military Identification Card.
  • Must record ID type, state, and ID number in dispensing log.
Electronic Signature Logs & CURES
  • Patient or representative must sign physical or electronic capture pad upon receipt.
  • Dispensings reported to CURES 2.0 (Controlled Substance Utilization Review and Evaluation System) within 1 business day.

2. Schedule II Partial Filling Rules (CARA Act & CA Law)

Under the Comprehensive Addiction and Recovery Act (CARA) and California pharmacy law, partial fills of Schedule II prescriptions are governed by explicit timeframes:

Partial Fill Scenario Legal Time Limit for Remaining Balance Special Stipulations
Requested by Patient or Prescriber Within 30 Days of Issue Date Total quantity dispensed across partial fills cannot exceed total written.
Pharmacy Out-of-Stock Emergency Within 72 Hours If remainder cannot be supplied in 72 hours, prescriber must be notified; balance is forfeited.
Terminally Ill / LTCF Patients Within 60 Days of Issue Date Rx must be annotated "Terminally Ill" or "LTCF Patient".

Module 5 • 20 Minutes Target

Quality Assurance, High-Alert Safety & Dispensing Math

ISMP high-alert drug safety, Tall Man lettering, near-miss reporting, and oral liquid reconstitution calculations.

1. High-Alert Medications & ISMP Safety Standards

High-alert medications bear a heightened risk of causing significant patient harm when dispensed in error. Key ISMP categories include:

Insulins & Antidiabetic Agents
High risk for severe hypoglycemia. Always double-check concentration (U-100 vs U-500).
Anticoagulants & Antithrombotics
Warfarin, Heparin, Enoxaparin. High risk for internal hemorrhage or clotting.
Opioids & Narcotics
Fentanyl patches, Morphine, Oxycodone. High risk for fatal respiratory depression.
Chemotherapeutic Agents
Methotrexate, Cyclophosphamide. Cytotoxic hazard; requires dedicated counting trays.

2. ISMP Tall Man Lettering Strategy

FDA and ISMP promote capitalization of unique letter combinations to distinguish look-ahead/sound-alike (LASA) drug pairs:

hydrOXYzine vs hydrALAZINE
predniSONE vs predniSOLONE
dopaMINE vs dobuTAMINE

3. Dispensing Math Lab: Oral Liquid Reconstitution

Reconstitution Math Principles:

When reconstituting antibiotic powders (e.g., Amoxicillin oral suspension), purified water is added in two portions to achieve final volume.

Prescription Order: Amoxicillin 250 mg / 5 mL Suspension. SIG: 5 mL PO TID × 10 days.
Total Volume Needed: 5 mL/dose × 3 doses/day × 10 days = 150 mL total.
Reconstitution Powder + 112 mL Purified Water = 150 mL Total Reconstituted Volume.
