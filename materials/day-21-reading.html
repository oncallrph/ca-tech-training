Controlled Substance Schedules & DEA Official Forms

Federal Controlled Substances Act (CSA), Schedules I-V classifications, DEA registration, and mandatory official forms.

1. The Federal Controlled Substances Act (CSA) Schedules

The Controlled Substances Act (CSA) categorizes drugs into five distinct schedules based on medical utility, abuse potential, and psychological or physical dependence risk:

Schedule Abuse Potential & Dependence Risk Medical Use Representative Drug Examples
Schedule I (C-I) High abuse potential; severe dependence risk. NO accepted medical use under federal law. Heroin, LSD, MDMA, Peyote (Marijuana remains C-I federally).
Schedule II (C-II) High abuse potential; severe physical/psychological dependence. Accepted medical use with severe restrictions. Fentanyl, Morphine, Oxycodone, Hydrocodone/APAP, Adderall, Ritalin, Methadone.
Schedule III (C-III) Moderate to low physical dependence or high psychological dependence. Accepted medical use. Tylenol w/ Codeine #3/#4, Buprenorphine (Suboxone), Ketamine, Anabolic Steroids.
Schedule IV (C-IV) Low potential for abuse and low risk of dependence. Accepted medical use. Alprazolam (Xanax), Lorazepam (Ativan), Diazepam (Valium), Zolpidem (Ambien), Tramadol.
Schedule V (C-V) Lowest abuse potential relative to C-IV. Accepted medical use. Cough syrups with Codeine (Robitussin AC), Pregabalin (Lyrica), Diphenoxylate/Atropine (Lomotil).

2. Essential DEA Forms & Inventory Rules

The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) requires specific forms for registration, procurement, theft reporting, and destruction:

DEA Form 224 Application for new DEA registration for retail pharmacies, hospitals, and practitioners. Must be renewed every 3 years (Form 224a).
DEA Form 222 Mandatory official paper or CSOS electronic form for ordering, transferring, or returning Schedule I and II drugs. Paper forms valid for 60 days.
DEA Form 106 Report of Theft or Significant Loss of controlled substances. Must notify the DEA and local police in writing within 1 business day of discovery.
DEA Form 41 Registrant Record of Controlled Substances Destroyed. Used when disposing of expired/damaged stock via reverse distributors.
Controlled Substance Inventory Requirements:
  • Initial Inventory: Taken on the exact date the pharmacy opens for business.
  • Biennial Inventory: Complete physical count taken at least every 2 years.
  • Count Rules: Schedule II requires an exact physical count. Schedules III-V require an exact count if bottle size > 1,000 tablets, or an estimated count if container holds 1,000 or fewer tablets.

Controlled Substance Prescriptions, Refills & Transfers

California Security Prescription form features, C-II restrictions, partial fills, emergency orders, and C-III through C-V refill limits.

1. California Security Prescription Form Mandatory Features

In California, all written controlled substance prescriptions must be produced on tamper-resistant forms printed by a state-approved security printer. Key security elements include:

Latent "VOID" Watermark Appears across the face of the prescription when photocopied or scanned.
Thermo-chromic Ink Heat-sensitive feature that changes color or disappears when rubbed or warmed by touch.
Security Printers ID & Lot Number Pre-printed DOJ batch number and approved printer identification code at the bottom.
Quantity Check-Boxes & Text Checkboxes indicating quantity ranges and written statement of exact numerical quantity.

2. Federal & California Refill & Partial Fill Rules

Schedule Refill Allowed? Expiration / Validity Period Partial Fill & Transfer Regulations
Schedule II (C-II) STRICTLY ZERO (0) REFILLS Expires 6 months from date written in CA. • Partial fill allowed under CARA Act up to 30 days from issue date.
• Terminally ill / LTCF patients: partial fills valid for 60 days.
• Transfers: NEVER transferable.
Schedule III & IV Max 5 refills within 6 months. Expires 6 months from date written. • Total refills cannot exceed 120-day supply total.
• Transferable ONE TIME between non-linked pharmacies (unlimited if shared real-time database).
Schedule V Refillable as authorized by prescriber. Expires 6 months in CA. Transferable ONE TIME unless sharing a real-time electronic database.
California Emergency C-II Oral Order Mandate: In an emergency, a prescriber may orally order a C-II drug. The pharmacy must receive a written hardcopy ("Hard Copy for Emergency Dispensing") from the prescriber postmarked or delivered within 7 days in California (7 days under CA law vs 7 days federal). If not received, pharmacy must notify the CA Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement (BNE) and DEA.

California Pharmacy Law, Board Rules & CURES System

California Code of Regulations (Title 16), CURES PDMP reporting, Pharmacist-in-Charge (PIC) roles, tech ratios, and mandatory consultation.

1. CURES: Prescription Drug Monitoring Program

CURES (Controlled Substance Utilization Review and Evaluation System) is California’s electronic database used to track Schedule II, III, IV, and V controlled substance prescriptions:

CURES Key Legal Mandates:
  • Mandatory Reporting: Pharmacies must transmit dispensing data for Schedules II-V to CURES within 1 working day of dispensing.
  • Prescriber Lookup: Prescribers are legally required to consult CURES prior to prescribing C-II through C-IV drugs for the first time and at least every 4 months thereafter.
  • Patient Safety: Helps identify potential drug-seeking behavior, doctor shopping, and dangerous drug interactions (e.g., concurrent opioid + benzodiazepine).

2. California Board of Pharmacy Key Personnel Ratios & Rules

Legal Parameter / Role Community / Retail Pharmacy Rule Hospital / Institutional Practice Rule
Technician-to-Pharmacist Ratio 1 Pharmacist = 1 Technician for the first pharmacist.
Each additional pharmacist allows 2 technicians (e.g., 2 RPh = 3 Techs; 3 RPh = 5 Techs). 		1 Pharmacist = 2 Technicians maximum in acute care settings.
Pharmacist-in-Charge (PIC) Designated pharmacist responsible for full legal compliance of the pharmacy. Cannot be PIC for more than 2 pharmacies (max 50 miles apart).
Mandatory Patient Consultation Mandatory for all new prescriptions or whenever dosage/strength/form changes. Technicians CANNOT consult—only offer consultation by RPh. Inpatient discharge meds require consultation or written offer.

Module 4 • 20 Minutes Target

Federal Healthcare Compliance Laws (HIPAA, CMEA, PPPA, DSCSA)

Protecting patient health information, Combat Methamphetamine limits, Poison Prevention packaging, and Drug Supply Chain track & trace.

1. Major Federal Health & Safety Statutes

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability & Accountability Act) Protects Protected Health Information (PHI). Requires Minimum Necessary rule, Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP), and secure destruction (shredding) of prescription labels/documents.
CMEA (Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act of 2005) Regulates Scheduled Listed Chemical Products (SLCPs: Pseudoephedrine, Ephedrine, Phenylpropanolamine). Behind-the-counter storage, mandatory photo ID logbook (kept 2 years), and strict purchase limits:
  • Daily OTC limit: 3.6 grams / day per purchaser.
  • 30-Day OTC limit: 9.0 grams / 30 days per purchaser.
  • Mail order 30-day limit: 7.5 grams / 30 days.
PPPA (Poison Prevention Packaging Act of 1970) Requires child-resistant safety caps (80% of children under 5 cannot open, 90% of adults can). Exemptions include sublingual Nitroglycerin, oral contraceptives, and signed blanket patient waivers.
DSCSA (Drug Supply Chain Security Act - "Track & Trace") Establishes electronic interoperable tracing of prescription drugs from manufacturer to dispenser using Transaction Information (TI), Transaction History (TH), and Transaction Statement (TS) retained for 6 years.

DEA Number Verification & Compliance Math Lab

DEA verification algorithms, pseudoephedrine base calculation formulas, and legal limit compliance.

1. DEA Number Structure & Verification Algorithm

A valid DEA number consists of 2 Letters followed by 7 Digits (e.g., AB1234563):

  • 1st Letter: Registrant type (A, B, F, M for Mid-level, X for Buprenorphine waiver).
  • 2nd Letter: First letter of practitioner's last name or business name.
  • Digits 1 through 7: Formula verification sequence.
DEA Verification Formula:
Step 1: Add 1st + 3rd + 5th digits = \(S_1\)
Step 2: Add (2nd + 4th + 6th digits) \(\times 2 = S_2\)
Step 3: Total Sum \(S_{\text{total}} = S_1 + S_2\)
Step 4: The last digit of \(S_{\text{total}}\) MUST match the 7th digit!
Sample Example: Dr. John Smith, DEA #: BS 1 4 2 9 6 3 5
• Step 1: \(1 + 2 + 6 = 9\)
• Step 2: \((4 + 9 + 3) \times 2 = 16 \times 2 = 32\)
• Step 3: \(9 + 32 = 41\)
• Step 4: Last digit is 1. The 7th digit in DEA is 5. \(\rightarrow 1 \neq 5 \rightarrow\) INVALID DEA NUMBER!

2. Pseudoephedrine Sales Limit Formula

\(\text{Max Allowed Tablets} = \frac{\text{Daily Limit (3,600 mg)}}{\text{mg of Pseudoephedrine per Tablet}}\)
